Why it's worth a look — a one-pager
Personal finance,
run like software

Lunch Money is a subscription budgeting app that treats your money like a dataset, not a dashboard someone else monetizes. You own the categorization logic, the API access, and the data — most competitors sell you convenience and quietly sell your transaction data to advertisers.

What makes it powerful

One real net worth, not five apps

Banks, credit cards, crypto wallets, investments, and manual assets (a house, a car) all roll into a single balance sheet — plus native support for 160+ currencies with historical FX rates, so a trip abroad or a foreign paycheck doesn't break the math.

Budgets that roll over, not reset

Unspent grocery budget carries into next month instead of vanishing on the 1st. Recurring bills are auto-detected and tracked separately from discretionary spend, so "did I actually overspend" is a real answer, not a guess.

Rules do the categorizing for you

Write a rule once ("Uber Eats → Dining, tag: takeout") and it applies retroactively and going forward. Split one transaction across categories, group receipts, attach a PDF/photo to any line — the ledger stays clean without manual busywork every week.

Shared, without losing your own view

Partners can share specific budgets or accounts while keeping personal spending private — built for real households, not a single-user assumption bolted onto a "family plan."

For us specifically

Lunch Money ships a full REST API — and an official MCP server, which is how this document got written: an AI agent (Claude) connected directly to a live Lunch Money account and could read balances, categories, and budgets, and could just as easily create transactions, apply rules, or split a receipt — no export/import, no screen-scraping. get_budget_summary, create_transactions, upsert_budget — it's a programmable ledger, not just an app with an API bolted on. That's the differentiator that matters to a technical audience: almost no budgeting tool exposes this much surface area.
